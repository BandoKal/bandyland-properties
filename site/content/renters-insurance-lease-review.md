Reviewed October 26, 2026. Source: the insurance agent's October 23 reply in the “Converting to rental” email thread, plus Jason's pool follow-up. No subsequent pool answer was visible when reviewed.

the agent recommends converting the homeowners policy to rental-dwelling coverage near renter occupancy and requiring renters insurance, with the owners listed as additional interests so he can notify them of cancellation/nonpayment. He recommended his agency; Jason prefers allowing other qualifying insurers. The email did not establish lease signing as the binding trigger or confirm current vacant-period coverage, the premium, pool requirements, or an effective date. Confirm these in writing before occupancy, without leaving a coverage gap.

An additional interest generally receives policy notices; it does not receive insured status or liability coverage. Renters insurance is not a guarantee that a landlord cannot be sued. [Progressive explanation](https://www.progressive.com/answers/interested-party-renters-insurance/). Tenant coverage and the owner's dwelling/liability policy serve different purposes. [State Farm coverage overview](https://www.statefarm.com/insurance/renters/coverage-options).

## Proposed clause concepts for professional review

Require renters insurance throughout the tenancy and renewals, from any insurer meeting agreed requirements. Specify a personal-liability limit after insurance/legal review. Require proof before possession, at each policy renewal, and upon reasonable written request. Request the owners as additional interests where available and prompt notice from the tenant of lapse/cancellation or material coverage change. Specify acceptable proof and responsible leaseholders. Do not require purchase from the agent or imply additional-interest status insures the owners.

These are drafting instructions, not a final enforceable lease clause. Leave the liability limit, notice language and remedies unresolved until reviewed in #14/#24.

## Discount or credit

No credit amount, eligibility rule or insurer-specific incentive has been approved. A fixed insurer-neutral credit for maintaining qualifying coverage is a possible concept to review, with cost recorded in #16 and consistent written eligibility. Do not promise it in marketing or a lease yet.

Tennessee's historical rebating bulletin addresses insurance-related inducements; it does not by itself determine whether this landlord arrangement is permissible. Have the lease reviewer and agent's compliance contact assess any purchase-linked credit, especially one tied to a particular agent. [Tennessee bulletin](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/commerce/documents/insurance/bulletins/021315_Rebating_Bulletin.pdf).
